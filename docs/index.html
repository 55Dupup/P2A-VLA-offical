Motivation and Method

Pretrained vision-language models provide visual context and instruction semantics for robot control, but a representation gap remains between these high-level features and continuous actions. Direct bridging mainly supervises final action predictions, while intermediate bridging adds targets such as plans, future observations, trajectories, or affordances. Both offer limited explicit guidance on how VLM priors should shape intermediate action representations throughout the policy.

P2A-VLA formulates action generation as a perception-guided representation refinement process. Stacked Perception-Guided Refinement (PGR) blocks incorporate hierarchical visual and textual VLM features into successive action representation updates. Two complementary training objectives guide this process: Progressive Representation Alignment (PRA) supports perceptually grounded, task-consistent refinement across layers; Local Structural Consistency (LSC) uses contextual affinities across samples to guide the local organization of action representations.

Progressive Representation Alignment (PRA)

Per-Layer Visual Grounding aligns each intermediate action representation with the corresponding visual features in a shared embedding space. Task-Guided Inter-Layer Refinement aligns representation updates between successive policy layers with task semantics. Together, these objectives encourage perceptually grounded and task-consistent refinement.

Local Structural Consistency (LSC)

Joint visual-language features provide a task-aware basis for estimating contextual affinities across samples. LSC constructs local perceptual neighborhoods from these affinities and applies affinity-weighted regularization, encouraging contextually related samples to have closer action embeddings. This complements PRA by guiding local organization across samples.

The alignment objectives and auxiliary projection heads are used only during training, adding no inference-time computation.

Implementation Details +

The reported model uses a Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone, 24 PGR blocks, rank-64 LoRA adaptation, and action chunks of length eight. Training combines an ℓ₁ action loss with PRA and LSC.

Ablations and Representation Analysis +

Contributions of PRA and LSC

Removing PRA reduces average LIBERO success from 98.5% to 96.1%; removing LSC reduces it to 97.3%. An MLP-only architecture achieves 93.8%, while PGR blocks without the two representation objectives achieve 95.6%. These ablations distinguish the architecture from the complementary training objectives.

The representation analysis examines the representations underlying these results. Per-layer alignment yields stronger perception–action correspondence across policy depth than final-layer-only alignment or no explicit alignment. With LSC, perceptually related samples form more coherent neighborhoods in action space.
